Hello! My name is Sachin Sisodiya and I am a PhD Economics Candidate at the University of Cincinnati. I am an applied microeconomist studying regulation, enforcement, and behavioral responses to policy in narcotics markets. My work spans both the supply side — how governments design incentives and enforce compliance in regulated drug production — and the demand side — how age of initiation, information interventions, and prescription monitoring shape substance misuse outcomes. I combine experimental, quasi-experimental, and structural methods across settings in India and the United States.

My job market paper studies moral hazard and diversion in India's licit opium sector — the world's only legal source of raw opium gum. I examine how a dynamic incentive in the regulatory regime resolves effort withdrawal among CPS farmers but inadvertently reintroduces the diversion channel that the regulation was designed to close. The diversion question has long been central to US-India narcotics policy — a bilateral initiative (JLOPS, 2001–2006) attempted to resolve it through agronomic measurement but could not; my paper approaches it through behavioral observation.

My broader research agenda spans both sides of narcotics markets. On the supply side, I study how regulatory design and enforcement shape farmer behavior and productive efficiency in India's licit opium sector, using spatial stochastic frontier analysis alongside the quasi-experimental methods in my JMP. On the demand side, I use randomized experiments to study how information interventions shape opioid misuse risk perception among young adults in the United States (with Rene Saran; R&R at Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics). I am also extending this demand-side agenda to study the impact of Prescription Drug Monitoring Programs (PDMPs) on gabapentin prescribing patterns using NeuroBlu electronic health records data.
